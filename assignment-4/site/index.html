Session 4 · how raw data becomes training data

Eight cleaning strategies, run for real on Indian-language data.

Session 4's single lesson: raw data is not training data until a reproducible pipeline has normalized it, filtered it, deduplicated it, scrubbed its PII, checked it for benchmark contamination, and recorded where every shard came from. We took a slice of ai4bharat/indic-align across our 12 Assignment-3 languages and ran all eight stages on it. Every number on this page is computed by the pipeline, not written by hand.

01

The dataset we clean, and why

The brieffind a 10-100M-token dataset (not the example) - ideally one you met in Session 3.

Before cleaning anything, we chose what to clean: ai4bharat/indic-align (mirror CharuAgarwal/indic-align, CC-BY-4.0) - the AI4Bharat instruction corpus, the same family as Sangraha that Session 3 uses as its running example. It covers all 12 Assignment-3 languages, stores each item as parallel translations in one column per language (so the column name is a claimed language we can test against detection), and ships a toxic subset for the safety check. We stream a slice inside the 10-100M-token requirement - the “take a part of it” the session explicitly allows.

Corpus

indic-align

Sources used: Dolly (15,011 items translated to 14 languages), Anudesh (native conversation), and the HH-RLHF + ToxicMatrix safety sets. Different sources arrive in different formats, which is what gives the ghost-tag check something to do.

Hold-out (never trained on)

mmlu-indic + trivia-qa-indic-mcq

Two Indic MCQ benchmarks (found in Session 3). They stay out of the corpus; we scan the corpus against them in the decontamination stage, so that check measures real leakage rather than describing the idea.

Tokenizer

MuRIL (197K)

Google's Indian-language tokenizer, covering all 17 scheduled languages incl. Urdu and Assamese - the real-world instance of the ~200K focused vocab we designed in Assignment 3, so the token counts and fertility are honest.

03

What came out: languages and fertility

The cleaned corpus, by detected language, and how efficiently each language tokenizes. Fertility is tokens per word - lower means the tokenizer represents the language in fewer pieces, which is the highest-leverage decision from our Assignment-3 design.

The Assignment-3 point, measured. Because MuRIL's 197K vocab covers all 12 of our languages, every one lands between roughly 1.2 and 2.3 tokens per word - including Perso-Arabic Urdu (1.21) and Assamese (1.54), which a smaller Indic vocabulary leaves far higher. Its Hindi 1.22 matches the target of the ~200K focused vocab we designed in Assignment 3: tokenizer coverage, not raw size, is what keeps fertility low.

05

Final statistics

The briefwhat are the final statistics?
Reproducible and traceable. The run uses a fixed seed, so the same input produces the same SHA-256; every shard carries a manifest recording its source, license, cleaning-script hash, token count and language mix, so nothing enters the corpus unlabeled. This is a cleaning exercise, not a trained model - the cleaned corpus is what would feed the India-first model designed in Assignment 3.
